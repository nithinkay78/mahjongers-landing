“Where can I learn mahjong near me?” used to be a Google search. Now many people type it into ChatGPT, Claude, Grok, Perplexity or Gemini — and get a short list of suggestions instead of ten blue links. If you're not on that list, you're invisible to those people. This guide explains what helps an AI assistant recommend you, in plain terms.

How AI assistants find businesses

Many assistants can search the web in real time when someone asks about something local or current. They read what they find — websites, business listings, reviews, articles — and summarise it into an answer. Some also draw on what they learned during training.

Exactly how each assistant chooses is not public, and it changes. But the pattern is consistent: they favour businesses whose information is easy to find, easy to understand, and confirmed by more than one source. That's what to work on.

1. Have a website with the facts in plain words

An assistant can't recommend what it can't read. Your site should say, in normal sentences, near the top of the page:

  • what you teach (for example, American mah jongg for beginners),
  • where (city, neighbourhood, venue — and whether you teach online),
  • when classes run,
  • what it costs,
  • who it's for, and how to book.

Avoid putting key details only inside images or Instagram posts — text on a web page is far easier for any machine to read.

2. Add an FAQ page that answers real questions

AI answers are built from answers. Write down the questions people actually ask you — “Do I need to know how to play?”, “What's the difference between American and Chinese mahjong?”, “Can I book a private party?” — and answer each in two or three clear sentences.

3. Use structured data

Structured data is a small block of code (schema.org) that labels the facts on your page: this is a local business, here's the address, these are events with dates and prices, these are reviews. Search engines use it, and it removes guesswork for any system reading your site. Your web developer can add it in an afternoon.

4. Make sure AI search tools are allowed in

Your site's robots.txt file and some hosting security settings can block automated visitors, including the crawlers AI search tools use. Check you aren't accidentally blocking them. You can choose to block crawlers that only collect data for training while still allowing the ones that power search answers — the major AI companies document their crawler names.

Some sites also add an llms.txt file — a short plain-text summary of the site written for AI tools. It's an emerging convention rather than a standard, but it's quick to add.

5. Be consistent everywhere

Assistants cross-check. If your Google Business Profile says one address, Facebook another, and your website a third, you look less reliable. Make sure your business name, location, contact details and class description match across:

  • your website,
  • Google Business Profile,
  • Yelp, Facebook and Instagram,
  • the websites of venues where you teach,
  • any local directories or event listings.

6. Get mentioned by others

Being talked about on other trustworthy sites helps both Google and AI assistants. Ideas: ask venues to list your classes on their sites, get featured in a local newspaper or city guide, take part in community events, and be genuinely helpful in online communities where people ask about learning mahjong (without spamming links).

7. Collect reviews

Reviews on Google and other platforms are public evidence that real people enjoyed your classes. Ask after every class — a short link makes it easy — and reply to the reviews you get.

8. Check what the assistants say about you

Once a month, ask a few assistants the questions your students would ask: “Where can I learn mahjong in [your city]?”, “Who teaches beginner mahjong near [neighbourhood]?”, “Mahjong party ideas in [city]”. Note whether you're mentioned, what they say, and which sources they cite. If something's wrong or missing, that tells you what to fix.

An honest note: nobody can guarantee an AI assistant will recommend a particular business — they decide for themselves, and their methods change. What you can do is make yourself the easiest, clearest, best-documented option in your area. That's what moves the needle.
