Plenty of mahjong teachers run their whole business from an Instagram account and a few WhatsApp groups. It works — up to a point. Then the DMs pile up, people ask the same five questions, and you realise you're relying on an app you don't control. Here's why a simple website of your own is worth it.

1. You own it

Your Instagram account belongs to Instagram. Algorithm changes can halve your reach overnight, and accounts do get locked or hacked. Your website and your domain are yours. Whatever happens on social media, people can always find you at yourname.com.

2. It answers questions while you're teaching

When do classes run? How much? Where? What should I bring? Do I need to know anything first? A good website answers all of this on one page, so people arrive at your DMs ready to book — or simply book without messaging at all.

3. People can book and pay without you

A booking page with card payments means seats fill at 11pm while you sleep. It also cuts no-shows, because people who've paid upfront turn up. No more chasing bank transfers or keeping a spreadsheet of who's coming.

4. Google sends you people who are ready to learn

Someone searching “mahjong lessons near me” or “learn mahjong in Dallas” already wants what you offer. Instagram posts rarely show up for those searches. A website with your city, your classes and your prices on it can — especially when it's paired with a Google Business Profile.

5. AI assistants can recommend you

More people now ask ChatGPT, Claude, Grok or Perplexity for recommendations. Those assistants tend to rely on clear, public information they can find on the open web. A website with plain facts about who you are, where you teach and what it costs gives them something to work with. We explain how in how to get your mahjong classes recommended by AI.

6. It makes you look established

Corporate clients booking a team event, venues considering a partnership, or a parent buying a gift card all want reassurance. A tidy website with your story, reviews and clear policies tells them you're a real business they can trust.

7. It turns one-off students into regulars

A website is the natural home for an email sign-up, gift cards, a schedule of upcoming classes, and a blog. Each of these brings past students back — and returning students are the steadiest part of any teaching business.

Instagram and a website work together

This isn't either/or. Instagram is where people discover you and get a feel for your table. Your website is where they get the facts and book. Point your bio link straight to your booking page, and use your posts to send people there.

The simplest version: three pages — a home page that says who you are and who it's for, a classes page with dates, prices and a booking button, and a contact page. That alone puts you ahead of most.
