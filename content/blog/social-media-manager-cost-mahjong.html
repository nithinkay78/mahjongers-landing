If you teach mahjong, host game nights or sell tiles and accessories, there comes a point where posting, editing and answering DMs eats into the time you'd rather spend at the table. So what does it actually cost to hand it off — and is it worth it?

Your three options

1. Do it yourself

Cost: your time. Realistically, a steady presence (three posts a week, Stories, replies) takes most people 4–8 hours a week once you include filming, editing and writing captions.

Best for: people who enjoy it, or who are just starting and want to learn what works before paying anyone.

2. Hire a freelancer

Typical cost: anywhere from a few hundred to around $1,500 a month in the US, depending on experience, the number of platforms and how much editing is involved.

Best for: people who can find someone reliable and are happy to brief them. The catch: most freelancers won't know mahjong, so you'll spend time explaining the game, the audience and what a “good” post looks like.

3. Hire an agency

Typical cost: often $1,000 to $5,000 or more a month for small businesses, usually with a minimum term.

Best for: larger businesses with ad budgets and several locations. For a solo teacher, a general agency is often more than you need.

What should be included?

Whoever you hire, make sure you know exactly what you're getting each month:

  • How many posts, and how many of them are Reels (video takes much longer to make).
  • Which platforms — Instagram and Facebook, plus TikTok or YouTube Shorts?
  • Whether Stories are included, and how often.
  • Who answers comments and DMs, and how quickly.
  • Whether paid ads are managed, and whether ad spend is separate (it almost always is).
  • How you approve content before it goes out.
  • What reporting you get, and whether there's a regular call.
  • That you keep ownership of your accounts at all times.

Will it pay for itself? A quick way to check

Work out what one extra student is worth to you, then how many you'd need each month to cover the cost.

Example: say a seat in your class is $50 and you run classes weekly. Two extra seats a week is about $400 a month; three extra seats is about $600. And if new students come back for a second or third class, each one is worth more than a single seat.

Use your own numbers. If better social media brings in a handful of new students a month — and frees up your evenings — it usually pays for itself. If you're already fully booked every week, you may only need a lighter plan to keep things ticking over.
