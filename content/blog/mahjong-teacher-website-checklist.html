Whether you're building your first site or wondering why your current one isn't bringing in bookings, run through this checklist. You don't need everything on day one — the first eight are the ones that matter most.

The essentials

  1. A clear headline. In one line: what you teach, who it's for, and where. “Beginner-friendly American mahjong classes in Charlotte.”
  2. A booking button above the fold. Visitors shouldn't have to scroll or hunt to book.
  3. Your class schedule. Dates, times, length, and how many seats are left if you can show it.
  4. Prices, in plain sight. Hiding prices makes people leave. Show them, along with what's included (sets, cards, snacks).
  5. Location details. Venue name, neighbourhood, parking or transit, and whether you also teach online.
  6. Who it's for. Total beginners? Improvers? Groups of friends? Corporate teams? Say so.
  7. Online payment. Taking card payments when people book cuts no-shows and back-and-forth messages.
  8. Mobile-friendly design. Most visitors will be on their phones. Check every page on yours.

The trust builders

  1. An “about” section with a real photo of you. People are booking time with a person — let them meet you.
  2. Reviews and testimonials. A few short quotes from real students, with first names.
  3. An FAQ. What to bring, cancellation policy, whether beginners are welcome, private bookings. This also helps AI assistants understand and recommend you.
  4. Clear policies. Cancellations, refunds, and a simple privacy policy if you collect emails.

The growth features

  1. Private events and parties page. Birthdays, showers and team events are often your best-paying bookings — give them their own page.
  2. Email sign-up. “Hear about new classes first.” Your email list is the cheapest way to fill future classes.
  3. Gift cards. Mahjong lessons make a lovely present, especially around the holidays.

Behind the scenes

A few things visitors won't see but that matter just as much:

  • Your own domain, registered in your name.
  • A Google Business Profile linked to your site.
  • Page titles and descriptions that include what you teach and your city.
  • Structured data so search engines and AI tools can read your business details and events.
  • Fast loading — compress big photos.
  • Simple analytics so you can see where visitors come from.

Quick test: show your site to a friend who's never been to your class. In ten seconds, can they tell what you offer, where, how much — and how to book? If not, start with items 1–4.
