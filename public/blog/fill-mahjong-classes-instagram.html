How to Fill Your Mahjong Classes Using Instagram

By the Mahjongers team · 22 October 2026 · 8 min read

You already know how to run a great table. The hard part is getting people to sit down at it. For most mahjong teachers and hosts, Instagram is where new students find you, check you out, and decide whether to book. This guide walks through a simple plan that turns your feed into a booking engine — without living on your phone.

1. Make your profile do the selling

Most people who find you will look at your profile for a few seconds before deciding whether to follow, message, or leave. Make those seconds count.

  • Name field: include what you do and where — for example, “Jane Lee · Mahjong Classes Austin”. The name field is searchable, so this helps local people find you.
  • Bio: one line on who it's for, one on what you offer, one call to action. “Beginner-friendly American mahjong · Weekly classes & game nights · Book your seat ↓”.
  • Link: point straight to your booking page, not your homepage. Every extra click loses people.
  • Highlights: four is plenty — Classes, Reviews, FAQ, Gallery.

2. Post the three things that fill seats

You don't need to post every day. You need a steady mix of three kinds of content:

Teach something small

Short Reels that solve one beginner problem: how to read the card, what a joker can and can't do, the Charleston in 30 seconds, three hands that are friendly for beginners. These get shared and saved, which puts you in front of people who don't follow you yet.

Show the table

People don't just book a lesson — they book an afternoon. Show the tiles, the snacks, the laughter, the moment someone calls their first Mahjong. Behind-the-scenes Stories (setting up, choosing the playlist, unboxing a new set) make you feel like someone worth spending time with.

Ask for the booking

Every week, say clearly what's coming up and how to join: “Saturday beginners' class — 4 seats left. Link in bio.” It feels obvious, but this is the post most teachers forget. If you never ask, followers assume you're full.

3. A realistic weekly rhythm

DayPost
MondayTeaching Reel (one quick tip)
WednesdayPhoto or carousel from a recent class, with a student quote
Friday“What's coming up” post with dates and seats left
Most days1–3 Stories: setting up, polls, reminders

Three posts a week, done consistently for three months, will beat a burst of daily posting that stops after two weeks.

4. Film once, post for a month

The biggest time-saver is batching. At one class or game night each month, prop your phone up and capture:

  • a wide shot of the full table for the first few minutes,
  • close-ups of hands, racks and a few interesting tile combinations,
  • you explaining one rule to camera (two or three takes),
  • a short “how was it?” clip from a happy student — always ask permission first.

That's enough raw material for a month of Reels and posts.

5. Turn comments and DMs into bookings

When someone comments “I've always wanted to learn!”, that's a lead. Reply publicly, then send a friendly DM with your next beginner date and the booking link. Keep a short saved reply for the questions you get most — price, what to bring, whether you need to know anything before coming.

Reply within a day if you can. A quick, warm answer is often the difference between a follower and a student.

6. Work with your neighbours

Collaborations put you in front of people who already trust someone else. Try a joint post with a local café that hosts your game night, a co-host with another teacher in a different city, or a giveaway with a tile or accessory maker. Instagram's Collab posts show on both accounts' feeds.

7. Watch three numbers, not thirty

Once a month, check:

  1. Profile visits — are more people checking you out?
  2. Link taps — are they heading to your booking page?
  3. Bookings that came from Instagram — just ask new students how they found you.

If visits are rising but link taps aren't, fix your bio and link. If link taps are rising but bookings aren't, look at your booking page and your prices.

Short version: a clear profile, three posts a week (teach, show the table, ask for the booking), quick replies to DMs, and one filming session a month.
