How Much Does a Website Cost for a Mahjong Business?

By the Mahjongers team · 22 October 2026 · 6 min read

Website prices range from almost nothing to tens of thousands of dollars, which makes it hard to know what's reasonable. Here's a practical breakdown for a small mahjong business — a teacher, a host, a club or a tile shop.

The costs everyone pays

  • Domain name: usually around $10–$25 a year for a .com.
  • Hosting: from free to $30+ a month depending on the platform.
  • Payment fees: card processors like Stripe charge a small percentage per transaction.
  • Booking or shop tools: some are free, some have a monthly fee or take a cut.

Option 1: Build it yourself

Typical cost: roughly $15–$50 a month for a website-builder plan, plus your time.

Good for: people who enjoy design and have a few weekends to spare. The risk is ending up with something that looks fine but doesn't bring in bookings — missing prices, a buried booking button, no SEO basics.

Option 2: Hire a freelancer

Typical cost: in the US, often somewhere between $1,500 and $5,000 for a small-business site, depending on pages and features.

Good for: people who want something custom and can find a reliable person. Ask what happens after launch — who updates it, who fixes it if it breaks, and whether you'll own everything.

Option 3: Hire an agency

Typical cost: often $5,000 to $20,000 or more.

Good for: larger businesses that need complex features, several locations or a full brand identity. For most solo teachers, it's more than you need.

Don't forget the monthly cost

A website isn't finished at launch. Class dates change, prices go up, software needs updating, and things occasionally break. Whether you pay a developer by the hour or a monthly care plan, budget for upkeep — or plan to do it yourself.

What you should get for your money

Whoever builds your site, make sure it includes:

  • a booking button and clear prices on the first screen,
  • a design that works well on phones,
  • basic SEO — page titles, descriptions, your city and what you teach,
  • a Google Business Profile connected to it,
  • structured data and plain-language FAQs so search engines and AI assistants can understand your business,
  • full ownership of your domain and content.

Our 15-point website checklist covers the rest.

Will it pay for itself?

Example: if a class seat is $50, a $1,250 website pays for itself after 25 extra bookings — about one extra student every two weeks for a year. Private events and repeat students get you there faster. Use your own numbers.
